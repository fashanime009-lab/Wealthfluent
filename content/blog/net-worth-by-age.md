## What net worth actually means

Your net worth is one simple sum:

**Net worth = everything you own − everything you owe**

What you own includes cash, savings, investments, retirement accounts, the value of your home and anything else you could sell. What you owe includes your mortgage, car loan, education loan, credit card balances and personal loans.

It's not your salary. Someone earning a lot with big loans can have a lower net worth than someone earning half as much with no debt. You'll remember that from [the janitor who died with $8 million](/blog/build-wealth-on-an-average-salary).

## Net worth by age: the newest US data

These figures come from the Federal Reserve's 2025 Survey of Consumer Finances, released on 9 October 2026. It's the most detailed wealth survey in the world, run every three years. Figures are per family, in 2025 US dollars.

| Age of head of family | Median net worth | Average (mean) net worth | Average ÷ median |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Under 35 | $33,000 | $135,400 | 4.1× |
| 35–44 | $155,600 | $681,900 | 4.4× |
| 45–54 | $253,700 | $1,244,500 | 4.9× |
| 55–64 | $411,900 | $1,878,000 | 4.6× |
| 65–74 | $431,300 | $1,897,600 | 4.4× |
| 75 and over | $504,900 | $1,962,200 | 3.9× |
| All families | $215,900 | $1,241,500 | 5.8× |

![Median net worth by age in the US, 2025 Survey of Consumer Finances](/blog/images/median-net-worth-by-age-2025.webp)

Three things jump out.

**1. Wealth climbs with age, all the way to the end.** Families aged 75 and over were the wealthiest group of all, with a median of $504,900. That group's median rose 37% in three years.

**2. Young families went backwards.** For families under 35, the median fell 23% and the average fell 33% between 2022 and 2025. The Fed says this was mainly the unwinding of business-equity gains young families had made between 2019 and 2022. If you're under 35 and feel like you're falling behind, the data says it's not just you.

**3. The "average" is a trap.** In every age group the average is about four to five times the median. That's not a typo, and it's the most important number on this page.

## Why the average lies to you

Imagine nine friends in a café, each with a net worth of $50,000. The average is $50,000. Then a billionaire walks in and orders a coffee. The average net worth in the room jumps to about $100 million.

Nobody in the café got a rupee richer. One person at the top dragged the average up.

That's exactly what happens in national wealth data. A small number of very wealthy households pull the **average** far above what a normal family has. The **median**, the family exactly in the middle, ignores the extremes. When a headline says "the average 40-year-old is worth $680,000", it's describing almost nobody.

**Always compare yourself to the median.**

## Net worth by age outside the US

Wealth surveys differ by country, so these numbers aren't directly comparable with the US table. Still, they give you a sense of what "typical" looks like where you live.

**United Kingdom.** The Office for National Statistics (Wealth and Assets Survey, April 2018 to March 2020) found a median household wealth of **£302,500**. Households headed by 25 to 34-year-olds had a median of **almost £77,000**. For 35 to 44-year-olds it was **nearly £200,000**, and for those aged 55 to State Pension age, **£553,400**. UK figures include private pension wealth, which is a big reason they look high.

**Around the world.** UBS's Global Wealth Report 2026 (end-2025 data) gives median wealth **per adult**, not per household, in US dollars:

| Country | Median wealth per adult (USD) |
| --- | --- |
| Australia | 210,783 |
| Canada | 147,811 |
| Switzerland | 145,555 |
| United Kingdom | 125,335 |
| Singapore | 96,434 |
| United States | 68,998 |
| Germany | 53,485 |

Two surprises here. First, the US is one of the richest countries by **average** wealth but sits far lower by **median**: the average trap again, at national scale. Second, per-adult figures look smaller than household figures because a household usually contains more than one adult.

**India.** UBS no longer publishes a median for every country. The last full table (2023 edition, end-2022 data) put India's median wealth per adult at about **$3,755**, and the world median at about **$8,654**. These are older figures, but they make one point clearly: if you have any positive net worth and some savings invested, you're already ahead of a large share of the world.

## So, are you behind?

Probably not as much as you think, and here's an honest way to check.

**Compare with the median for your age, not the average.** If you're 38 with a net worth of $90,000, you're below the US median for your age group ($155,600). That's useful information, not a verdict. If you were at $40,000 three years ago, you're moving fast in the right direction, and that's the comparison that matters most.

**Look at the direction, not the snapshot.** A 30-year-old with a large education loan can have a negative net worth and still be doing everything right. Net worth early in life is mostly about the trend: is the number rising every year?

**Use a savings-based target alongside it.** Fidelity's widely quoted guideline is to aim for about **1× your salary saved for retirement by 30, 3× by 40, 6× by 50, 8× by 60 and 10× by 67**. ## How to grow your net worth, at any age

There's no secret. There are only a few levers, and you control all of them.

1. **Pay off expensive debt first.** Clearing a credit card at 36% interest is a guaranteed 36% return. No investment beats that.
2. **Invest automatically every month.** Even small amounts compound. [Investing a fixed amount monthly](/blog/how-much-to-invest-to-become-a-millionaire) is how most ordinary people reach seven figures.
3. **Raise your savings rate with every pay rise.** Upgrade your future before you upgrade your lifestyle.
4. **Build an emergency fund.** It stops one bad month from turning into new debt. Use the [Emergency Fund Calculator](/emergency-fund-calculator) to size yours.
5. **Track your net worth once or twice a year.** Not daily, not weekly. You're watching a long-term trend, not a stock ticker.
