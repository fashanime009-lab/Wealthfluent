The typical Canadian family had a net worth of **$519,700** in 2023. That's according to Statistics Canada's Survey of Financial Security, the country's main wealth survey, which runs every few years. It's up 36% from 2019, after adjusting for inflation.

But a single number tells you very little. A 30-year-old renter and a 60-year-old homeowner with a pension live in different financial worlds. So here is net worth by age, what changed since 2019, and a quick way to see where you stand.

## Median net worth by age in Canada (2023)

| Age of main income earner | Median net worth 2019 | Median net worth 2023 | Average net worth 2023 | Change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Under 35 | $56,400 | $159,100 | $459,800 | +182% |
| 35 to 44 | $270,800 | $409,300 | $753,500 | +51% |
| 45 to 54 | $602,000 | $675,800 | $1,150,800 | +12% |
| 55 to 64 | $797,000 | $873,400 | $1,347,300 | +10% |

*Median net worth of families and unattached individuals, in constant 2023 dollars. Source: Statistics Canada, Survey of Financial Security 2023, [Table 1](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/241029/t001a-eng.htm). Changes are worked out from the table.*

*Survey figures have a margin of error. The gains for ages 45 to 54 and 55 to 64 are within it, so treat those two changes as roughly flat. The gains for the other age groups are clear.*

![Median net worth by age in Canada, 2019 vs 2023](/images/pages/median-net-worth-by-age-canada-2023.webp)

We use the **median**, the family exactly in the middle, because the average is pulled up by a small number of very wealthy families. Statistics Canada itself warns that its survey under-counts the very rich, which is one more reason the median is the fairer benchmark.

For reference, the average Canadian family's net worth was $995,900 in 2023, almost twice the median, because a small number of very wealthy families pull the average up.

## Three things the data shows

**1. Young Canadians made the biggest gains.** Families whose main earner was under 35 saw their median net worth almost triple, to $159,100. Statistics Canada puts the real increase at 179%. Young homeowners reached a median of $457,100, while young renters had $44,000.

**2. Wealth peaks just before retirement.** Families aged 55 to 64 had the highest median of any age group, at $873,400. The median is lower for families aged 65 and older, at $738,900.

**3. A home and a workplace pension change everything.** Among families aged 55 to 64, those with both a home and an employer pension had a median net worth of **$1.4 million**. Those with neither had **$11,900**.

| Families aged 55 to 64 (2023) | Median net worth |
| --- | --- |
| Own a home and have an employer pension | $1.4 million |
| Own a home, no employer pension | $914,000 |
| Employer pension, no home | $359,000 |
| Neither | $11,900 |

![A home and a pension: how they change net worth at 55 to 64 in Canada](/images/pages/canada-net-worth-home-and-pension.webp)

If you have neither, that's not a verdict. It just means your savings and investments have to do the work a pension and home equity do for others. Tools like an RRSP or TFSA, invested steadily, are how many Canadians close that gap.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the average (median) net worth by age in Canada?
The median net worth in 2023 was $159,100 for families under 35, $409,300 for 35 to 44, $675,800 for 45 to 54, $873,400 for 55 to 64, and $738,900 for 65 and older, according to Statistics Canada.

### What is the median net worth of a Canadian family?
$519,700 in 2023, up from $381,100 in 2019 after adjusting for inflation.

### What is a good net worth at 30 in Canada?
The median for families whose main earner is under 35 is $159,100. Young homeowners had a median of $457,100 and young renters $44,000, so whether you own a home matters a lot at this age.

### What is a good net worth at 40 in Canada?
The median for families aged 35 to 44 is $409,300.

### Which province has the highest net worth?
British Columbia, with a median of $773,500 in 2023, followed by Ontario at $665,600.

## The bottom line

The typical Canadian family is worth about half a million dollars, and young families have gained the most since 2019. But a home and a workplace pension make a gap of more than a million dollars by their late 50s and early 60s. Whatever your starting point, the lever you control is the same: invest steadily, and give it time.
