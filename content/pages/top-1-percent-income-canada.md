To be in Canada's top 1% of earners, you needed a total income of at least **$293,800** in 2023. That's according to Statistics Canada's latest tax-filer data, released in October 2025. To reach the top 0.1%, you needed **$930,100**.

For context, the average Canadian tax filer had a total income of **$59,900**. So the door to the top 1% opens at almost **5 times** the average income, and the average person inside earns about **10 times** it.

## Canada's top-income cutoffs, 2023

| Group | You need to earn at least | People in the group |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Top 1% | $293,800 | 310,660 |
| Top 0.1% | $930,100 | about 31,000 |
| Top 0.01% | $3,487,600 | about 3,100 |

*Total income before tax, 2023. Source: Statistics Canada, High-income tax filers in Canada (released 31 October 2025). The 0.1% and 0.01% headcounts are approximate, worked out from the top 1% total.*

"Total income" counts more than salary. It includes employment and self-employment income, investment income, pensions, support payments, other taxable income and government transfers.

## What Canada's top 1% looks like

The numbers behind the cutoff are more interesting than the cutoff itself:

- **The average top 1% earner made $606,000** in 2023, and the median was $402,900. A handful of very high incomes pull the average up, just as they do in [net worth data](/blog/net-worth-by-age).
- **Most of it is still a paycheque.** Wages and salaries made up 61.5% of top 1% income in 2023, down from 64.3% in 2016. The rest comes mostly from investments and business income.
- **They're mostly in their 50s.** The median age of the top 1% was 53.
- **About one in four is a woman.** Women made up 26.4% of the top 1% in 2023, up from 25.6% a year earlier.
- **They earn 10.1% of all income and pay 22.1% of all income tax.** The average top 1% earner paid $235,600 in federal and provincial income tax.
- **It isn't permanent.** Only 70.9% of people in the top 1% were still there the following year. Roughly three in ten drop out each year as bonuses, business profits or one-off gains come and go.

## Top 1% income by province

Statistics Canada reports the **average income of the top 1%** in each province. These are averages, not entry cutoffs:

| Province | Average total income of the top 1% (2023) | Average wages and salaries of the top 1% |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alberta | $644,400 | $413,900 |
| Ontario | $623,200 | $396,200 |
| British Columbia | $596,300 | $344,200 |
| Quebec | $570,100 | $325,700 |

Alberta's top 1% also had the highest average wages, at $413,900. Ontario has the most stable top 1%: 72.5% of its top earners stayed in the group the following year, the highest of any province.
