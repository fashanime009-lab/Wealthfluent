To be in the richest 1% of adults in the world, you need a net worth of **about $1 million**. Net worth means everything you own minus everything you owe. It isn't your income.

That bar is lower than most people expect, and the bar for the top half is far lower still: an adult with roughly **$15,000** to their name is already wealthier than half the world.

## Why $1 million is the line

The UBS Global Wealth Report counts the world's US-dollar millionaires every year. Its latest count is about **57.5 million**. In the 56 countries UBS studies in detail, which hold about 92% of the world's wealth, millionaires are **1.5% of adults**. Add the billions of adults in the poorer countries outside that sample, and millionaires come to roughly **1 in every 100 adults on Earth**.

So the global top 1% starts at around $1 million. If you own a paid-off home in a rich country plus a decent retirement account, you may be closer than you think.

## The world's wealth in one picture

![1.5% of adults hold 48% of the world's wealth](/images/pages/global-wealth-pyramid.webp)

Wealth is spread very unevenly. The few adults with more than $1 million hold almost half of all personal wealth. The 42% of adults with less than $10,000 hold under 1% of it.

That's why the climb looks so strange. Modest savings by Western standards already put you above the world's middle, but reaching the very top takes many times more.

## Rich country, ordinary wealth

Living in a rich country doesn't make the typical person rich. In the United States, the **average** wealth per adult is about $696,000, but the **median**, the adult exactly in the middle, is about $69,000. A small number of very wealthy people pull the average far above what most people own.

That's why we use the median, and why comparing yourself with "the average" can make you feel poorer than you are. For country-level benchmarks by age, see [net worth by age](/blog/net-worth-by-age).

## How people actually reach $1 million

Most millionaires didn't get there through a giant salary. They got there through time and steady investing. These rules don't change from year to year:

- **Start early.** $500 a month invested at a 7% yearly return grows to about **$610,000 in 30 years** and about **$1.3 million in 40 years**. The last 10 years add more than the first 30.
- **Save a fixed share of every pay rise.** Spending grows to match income unless you decide otherwise.
- **Own things that grow.** Most millionaire wealth sits in homes, retirement accounts and investments, not in bank savings.
- **Avoid high-interest debt.** Card debt at 20% or more undoes years of investing.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much net worth do you need to be in the global top 1%?
About $1 million. Roughly 57.5 million adults own over $1 million, which is about 1 in every 100 adults in the world.

### How much do you need to be in the top 10% globally?
About $150,000 in net worth, based on our estimate from the UBS Global Wealth Report.

### What net worth puts you in the richer half of the world?
About $15,000. Nearly half the world's adults own less than $10,000.

### Is the global top 1% the same as the top 1% in my country?
No. In rich countries the national top 1% starts far higher than $1 million. The global top 1% compares you with every adult on Earth, including billions with very little wealth.

### Is net worth the same as income?
No. Net worth is what you own minus what you owe. A retiree with a paid-off home can be in the global top 10% on a small income. To compare your income instead, use the [Global Income Percentile calculator](/income-percentile).
