It's to check five numbers against honest benchmarks for your age. It takes about two minutes.

Score one point for each check you pass. **4–5 points: you're ahead. 2–3: on track, with gaps to fix. 0–1: time for a plan,** and the order below tells you where to start.

## Check 1: Is your net worth in line with people your age?

Net worth is everything you own minus everything you owe. The best benchmark is the **median**, the household exactly in the middle, not the average, which a few very rich families drag up.

The newest US figures come from the Federal Reserve's 2025 Survey of Consumer Finances, released in October 2026. The typical family headed by someone under 35 had **$33,000**. At 35–44 it was **$155,600**, and at 55–64 it was **$411,900**.

Outside the US, the numbers differ because surveys differ:

- **United Kingdom:** households headed by 25 to 34-year-olds had a median of almost **£77,000**, and 35 to 44-year-olds nearly **£200,000** (ONS, 2018–2020, including pensions).
- **Canada:** families whose main earner was under 35 had a median of **C$159,100**, and all families **C$519,700** (Statistics Canada, 2023).

**Pass if:** your net worth is positive and higher than it was a year ago. Being above the median for your age is a bonus, not the bar.

## Check 2: Are your retirement savings on pace?

Fidelity's widely used guideline is to have about **1× your salary saved for retirement by 30, 3× by 40, 6× by 50, 8× by 60 and 10× by 67**. It assumes you start saving at 25 and retire at 67.

**Pass if:** your retirement savings are at or near the multiple for your age. If you're behind, the gap closes faster than you'd think once you raise your monthly amount, because compounding does more of the work each year.

See your own numbers with the [Retirement Calculator](/retirement-calculator), or read [how much to save for retirement by age](/blog/how-much-to-save-for-retirement-by-age).

## Check 3: Are you saving at least 15% of your income?

Fidelity suggests saving **15% of your pre-tax income** for retirement each year, including any employer contribution. Your savings rate matters more than your salary. Someone earning 50,000 who saves 20% builds wealth faster than someone earning 100,000 who saves 5%.

**Pass if:** you save 15% or more. Under 10%? Start by moving 1% more each time your pay rises. You won't feel it, and in five years you'll be there.

## Check 4: Could you survive three months without income?

An emergency fund is money set aside for a job loss, a medical bill or a broken car. The common guideline is **3 to 6 months of essential expenses**, kept somewhere safe and easy to reach.

**Pass if:** you have at least 3 months covered. This one matters more than it looks: without a buffer, one bad month turns into credit card debt, which undoes every other check.

Find your target with the [Emergency Fund Calculator](/emergency-fund-calculator).

## Check 5: Are you free of high-interest debt?

A home loan at a reasonable rate is normal at most ages. Credit card balances and expensive personal loans are different. Credit card interest is often **above 20% a year**, and in some countries over 36%. No investment reliably beats that.

**Pass if:** you carry no credit card balance from month to month and have no high-interest personal loans.

Behind on this one? Fix it first. Paying off a 24% card is a guaranteed 24% return.

## What to fix first if you're behind

Work through the checks in this order. Each one protects the next:

1. **Clear high-interest debt.** It's an emergency that compounds against you.
2. **Build 3 months of expenses as an emergency fund.** It stops new debt from appearing.
3. **Raise your savings rate toward 15%.** Automate it so it happens before you can spend it.
4. **Let retirement savings and net worth grow.** Checks 1 and 2 take care of themselves when 3, 4 and 5 are in place.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I know if I'm doing well financially for my age?
Check five things: net worth compared with the median for your age, retirement savings compared with your salary, a savings rate of at least 15%, an emergency fund of 3 to 6 months, and no high-interest debt. Passing four or five means you're ahead of most people your age.

### Where should I be financially at 30?
A common benchmark is about one year's salary saved for retirement by 30, a positive and rising net worth, 3 to 6 months of expenses in an emergency fund, and no credit card debt. The typical US family headed by someone under 35 has a net worth of $33,000.

### Where should I be financially at 40?
Fidelity's guideline is about three times your salary saved for retirement by 40. The median US family aged 35 to 44 has a net worth of $155,600.

### Is it too late to catch up financially at 40 or 50?
No. A higher savings rate, cleared debt and steady investing can close much of the gap, and many people do most of their wealth building after 40. The key is to start with the checks you're failing, in the order above.

### What is a wealth age calculator?
It compares your "financial age", based on your savings rate and net worth, with your real age. A financial age below your real age means your money habits are ahead of where they need to be.

## The bottom line

You're not behind because a friend posted a new car. You're behind only if your own five numbers say so, and the fix is usually the same: clear expensive debt, build a buffer, and save a little more each year. Check again in twelve months. The person to beat is you, last year.
