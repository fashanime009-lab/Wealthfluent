I want the design to actually be beautiful and colorful, not just "not
generic." I think we over-corrected toward restraint, treating minimalism
as the only safe way to look trustworthy for a finance site — that's not
true. Look at Wise, Monzo, or Copilot Money for reference: real fintech
products that are bold, colorful, and have genuine personality without
losing credibility.

Specifically add:

1. A real color system — not one accent color (emerald) used sparingly
   on a mostly neutral page. Give different content categories (loan
   tools, investment tools, retirement tools) their own distinct color
   identity, the way the category badges already hint at but don't
   commit to.

2. Full-bleed color variation between sections — not "cream, one dark
   green band, cream again." Vary background tone more as the page
   scrolls, so it feels like moving through distinct spaces.

3. A genuinely considered chart/data color palette — not just green for
   "winning" and amber for "losing," but real visual richness in how
   data is displayed.

4. Typographic personality — a distinctive display treatment for big
   numbers and headlines, something with real character.

5. Tasteful custom illustration or iconography where it helps — not the
   generic gradient blob we removed, but something specific and
   well-crafted, if you think it adds value.

Keep everything we got right before: no gradient-badge/glassmorphism
clichés, the real chart in the hero, the instrument-panel tilt
interaction, real hierarchy. I just don't want "restrained" to be the
same thing as "plain." Propose an updated concept before building.
